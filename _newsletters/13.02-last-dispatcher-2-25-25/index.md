<h3 class="newsletter-article-title" id="article-3"><a href="https://www.understandingai.org/p/these-experts-were-stunned-by-openai?utm_campaign=email-half-post&amp;r=3y2r5&amp;utm_source=substack&amp;utm_medium=email" rel="noopener noreferrer">These experts were stunned by OpenAI Deep Research</a> </h3>
<p>T. Lee, Feb. 21, "Earlier this month, OpenAI <a href="https://openai.com/index/introducing-deep-research/" rel="noopener noreferrer">released a new product</a> called Deep Research. Based on a variant of the (still unreleased) o3 reasoning model, Deep Research can think for even longer than conventional reasoning models—up to 30 minutes for the hardest questions. And crucially, it can search the web, allowing it to gather information about topics that are too new or obscure to be well covered in its training data.  </p>
<p>I wanted to thoroughly test Deep Research out, so I solicited difficult questions from a random sample of Understanding AI readers. One of them was Rick Wolnitzek, a retired architect who runs the website <a href="https://www.architekwiki.com/" rel="noopener noreferrer">Architekwiki</a>. Wolnitzek asked for a detailed building code checklist for a 100,000-square-foot educational building….." <a href="https://www.understandingai.org/p/these-experts-were-stunned-by-openai?utm_campaign=email-half-post&amp;r=3y2r5&amp;utm_source=substack&amp;utm_medium=email" rel="noopener noreferrer">Read more</a> </p><p class="newsletter-commentary">Hmmmm… This is really interesting; however, I continue to wonder as to the extent of its value to whom? Spending 30 minutes on even just an ordinary laptop is executing about 1,800 times E11 Flops or 1.8E14 flops. That's a lot of computation. Didn't Google/Chrome tell us that it had found (and sorted) a gazillion sites/links in the fraction of a second of its search for our query; albeit those on top/many/most/? are ads or sponsored results, but still… a gazillion in less than a second! Wow. What is Deep Research doing during those 30 minutes????? And how is it sorting the noise from the signal biased one way and the signal biased the other way. Do its results earn a "<a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Good_Housekeeping" rel="noopener noreferrer">Good Housekeeping Seal of Approval</a>"??? Alain  </p>
<h3 class="newsletter-article-title" id="article-4"><a href="https://www.planetizen.com/features/134356-has-progress-travel-safety-run-road" rel="noopener noreferrer">Has Progress on Travel Safety Run Off the Road?</a> </h3>
<p>S. Polzin Feb. 21, "…As is the case with airline accidents, many factors contribute to roadway vehicle crashes. The roadway infrastructure, the vehicle, the weather and climatic conditions, the driver, and emergency response all play a role. … "the driver" is far down on the list, instead of FIRST??? I thought …" &gt;90% of crashes involved driver misbehavior"…  </p>
<p>…Shaping driver behavior  </p>
<p>Safety initiatives that attempt to shape drivers' behaviors that contribute to accidents have been less aggressive. A colleague protests that these should be labeled as misbehaviors and not accidents, as the data suggest huge numbers of fatalities are associated with driver misbehaviors. Perhaps that is why safety professionals use the terminology <a href="https://www.planetizen.com/node/80560/does-it-matter-if-we-call-crashes-accidents" rel="noopener noreferrer">crashes and not accidents</a>.  </p>
<p>There are certainly rules influencing behaviors — for example, cell phone and seat belt use — but compliance and enforcement aren't sufficient to keep these factors from being significant contributors to crash fatalities. Americans have shown reluctance to be more aggressive in initiatives to influence driver behavior.….." <a href="https://www.planetizen.com/features/134356-has-progress-travel-safety-run-road" rel="noopener noreferrer">Read more</a> </p><p class="newsletter-commentary">Hmmmm… This is a thoughtful and important article on roadway safety.  </p>
<p class="newsletter-commentary">My take is… Very nice!!!!  </p>
<p class="newsletter-commentary">As you know… if one really wants to address road safety one must address driver misbehavior. Thinking infrastructure and/or gizmos are/is going to make a dent is naive/doomed/…  </p>
<p class="newsletter-commentary">It would be nice if education could do it, but education isn't that good.  </p>
<p class="newsletter-commentary">Either we put up with it (which is what we've grown accustomed to) or we bring down the enforcement hammer and don't allow it… speed governors, ignition interlocks, jail time, claw-back of trust funds. And warning labels that resemble those on cigarette packs for vehicles that operate on any public street even if they are red with a yellow emblem These kill!!!  </p>
<p class="newsletter-commentary">Plus no more advertising that they climb great walls and travel down river beds and appeal to our most unmentionable desires.  </p>
<p class="newsletter-commentary">Plus, while we're at it, let's not burden driverless cars with solving the driver mis-behavior problem, because mis-behaving drivers will never use them. They love to drive  </p>
<p class="newsletter-commentary">Let's be happy and welcoming that driverless cars (and nearly driverless cars) today solve the driver mis-behavior problem for folks who really need a ride. Let's welcome (allow them on our streets and don't play stupid games with them) and be supportive of them because they don't misbehave. Why are they (Waymo's &amp; GM/cruises &amp; ???) illegal anyway? They today are substantially way safer than the misbehaving drivers that are being tolerated and coddled.  </p>
<p class="newsletter-commentary">No texting (phones know you are driving. Make the phone maker liable. They'll stop asking if you are driving (if their "AI" is any good)).  </p>
<p class="newsletter-commentary">No excessive speeding… Make the manufacturer liable. The car knows how fast it is going and knows where it is and knows the weather. The speed limit is not a secret.  </p>
<p class="newsletter-commentary">It is a safety defect if the car is allowed to speed excessively. Make all car manufacturers recall all their cars and fix it. Make them liable if excessive speeding is the cause of the crash.  </p>
