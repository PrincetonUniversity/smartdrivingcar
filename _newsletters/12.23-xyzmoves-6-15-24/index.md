<p class="newsletter-commentary">When human driven, these incidents aren't mentioned to anyone since it is well-known that the insurance implications would vastly outweigh eating the cost to fix. Human drivers aren't stupid! 😊</p>
<p class="newsletter-commentary">Since they are making a big deal out of it, their explanation and "fix" seems to be perplexing and raises questions about Waymo's service concept:</p>
<ul>
<li>What was it about "Giving this rider a ride" had Waymo's routing algorithm telling this vehicle to go down this alley?</li>
<li>Why is this alley even in the feasible set of their network? Who was it going to pick up where, that was so darn important that it chose to use this alley?</li>
<li>What aspect of their "<a href="https://nymag.com/intelligencer/2016/08/trolley-problem-meme-tumblr-philosophy.html" rel="noopener noreferrer">Trolley Problem</a>" conundrum resolution algorithm chose "hit that pole" rather than "hit this other thing"?</li>
<li>Don't Waymos have Automated Emergency Braking that is better than what <a href="https://www.nhtsa.gov/press-releases/nhtsa-fmvss-127-automatic-emergency-braking-reduce-crashes#:~:text=Today%2C%20the%20U.S.%20Department%20of,light%20trucks%20by%20September%202029." rel="noopener noreferrer">NHTSA has recently mandated</a>? It hit a stationary object at 8mph! That should simply NOT happen unless the alternative was really bad… in which case the system worked well and should NOT be changed.</li>
<li>Do all stationary objects need to be in Waymo's HD map database, else they can be hit? If that is the case, then label all objects as non-stationary. Waymo has an excellent history of avoiding moving objects (none of which are in their HD map data base.) Use that!</li>
</ul>
