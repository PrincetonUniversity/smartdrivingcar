<h3 class="newsletter-article-title" id="article-1"></h3>
<h3 class="newsletter-article-title" id="robot-cars--with-no-human-driver--could-hit-california-roads-next-year"><a href="http://www.latimes.com/business/autos/la-fi-hy-dmv-autonomous-regulations-20170310-story.html" rel="noopener noreferrer">Robot cars — with no human driver — could hit California roads next year</a></h3>
<p>R. Mitchell, Mar 10, "California is
back on the map as a state that's
serious about welcoming driverless
cars.Truly driverless cars — vehicles
with no human behind the wheel, and
perhaps no steering wheel at all — are
headed toward California streets and
highways starting in 2018...</p>
<p><a href="https://www.dmv.ca.gov/portal/dmv/detail/vr/autonomous/auto" rel="noopener noreferrer">The regulations</a> lay out "a clear
path for future deployment of
autonomous vehicles" in California,
said Bernard Soriano, deputy director
at the Department of Motor
Vehicles...." <a href="http://www.latimes.com/business/autos/la-fi-hy-dmv-autonomous-regulations-20170310-story.html" rel="noopener noreferrer">Read more</a> </p><p class="newsletter-commentary">Hmmm...
Congratulations Bernard!  This is
fantastic news on the road to
providing high-quality mobility
for all.  It squarely addresses
the fundamental need to efficiently
re-position vehicles so that they
can get to even those who can't
drive.  This is a real turning
point for automated vehicles from
self-driving toys for the 1% to
affordable, environmentally
friendly mobility for everyone.
Alain</p>
