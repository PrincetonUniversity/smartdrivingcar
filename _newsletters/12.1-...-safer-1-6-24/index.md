<p>Editorial: Time to let Value do the talking and put safety and, especially technology, in the background!</p>
<p>What a year. On a personal level, it was absolutely fantastic. Great sabbatical. Traveled through Italy and Kenya, three trips to Boca Chica: spring break with Elizabeth &amp; Helen, launch of <a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-1wcilQ58hI" rel="noopener noreferrer">StarShip FT1</a> with Jerry He, and launch of <a href="https://www.spacex.com/launches/mission/?missionId=starship-flight-2" rel="noopener noreferrer">StarShip FT2</a> with Elizabeth and 9 students from my best class ever. Plus the writing of "<a href="https://shop.elsevier.com/books/the-real-case-for-driverless-mobility/kornhauser/978-0-443-23685-3" rel="noopener noreferrer">The Real Case for Driverless Mobility</a>" with Michael Sena, 51 issues of the SDC eLetter, and 53 SDC PodCast/ZoomCasts with Fred Fishkin. I am so blessed.</p>
<p>For SmartDrivingCars, it was truly a "Tale of Two Cities"… It was the best of times: From my perspective, Driverless passed the "proof-of-technology" test by having both Waymo and Cruise deliver Driverless rides without Smoke&amp;Mirrors to the public in two Operational Design Domains, Phoenix/Chandler &amp; San Francisco. Since nothing is perfect, it wasn't either, but it was more than perfect enough to move on to the next step of having the opportunity to begin delivering value. The imperfections were just a very few very minor delays in a few chaotic situations that regularly occur in lively cities, which are completely normal in the learning process of trying to use a new tool to do good and as is all too common in life: the learning of a hard lesson the hard way, for which the silver lining ends up being… this lesson is now learned and its likelihood of being repeated is now even more extremely small. That was the fantastic good news for 2023.</p>
<p>But it is a tale of Two cities. The bad news is that not only has there been essentially no advancement in the "proof-of-market" for Driverless mobility. We may also have even moved backwards. I, with Michael Sena, will claim that the Driverless "industry" has been barking up the wrong tree and that became all too obvious in 2023. What is in the forefront has no hope sufficient value for sufficient customers. A viable business case isn't on the table. These systems have been put forth as Uber/Lyft copycats without that in comparison are also-rans. They aren't substantially "safer", if at all "safer"; they aren't substantially "faster", if at all "faster"; they aren't substantially "cleaner", if at all "cleaner"; they aren't substantially "…er", if at all "…er". They're also-rans. Their only hope lies in their opportunity to be substantially more affordable.</p>
<p>The Driverless winner will be the company that creates an Operational Design Domain that serves with high-quality the mobility aspirations of customers that most value affordability. Alain</p>

<h3 class="newsletter-article-title" id="article-1"><a href="https://www.theverge.com/2024/1/5/24025239/aurora-autonomous-truck-finalize-design-continental-2027" rel="noopener noreferrer">Aurora finalizes design of self-driving trucks it will make with Continental</a></h3>
<p>Andrew Hawkins, Jan. 5, "Autonomous vehicle company Aurora Innovation announced the finalization of the design, architecture, and hardware for its Aurora Driver. The technology will power its future lineup of driverless semi trucks that it's producing with German auto supplier Continental starting in 2027.</p>
<p>Aurora says it still plans on deploying a handful of fully driverless trucks by the end of 2024. But having reached the milestone of finalizing the design of its self-driving hardware and software stack, the company says it's poised to deploy "thousands" of trucks in just three years.</p>
<p>Aurora — which was founded in 2017 by alumni of Uber, Tesla, and Waymo — has said it plans to deploy 20 fully autonomous trucks this year, with an eye on expanding to about 100 trucks in 2025 and eventually selling to other companies… "<br/>
<a href="https://www.theverge.com/2024/1/5/24025239/aurora-autonomous-truck-finalize-design-continental-2027" rel="noopener noreferrer">Read More</a> </p><p class="newsletter-commentary">Hmmmm…. This is actually really good news because it is a necessary pathway to a "proof-of-market". Hopefully this design hides the technology and focuses on the value proposition of more efficient, lower cost and more affordable truck movements by the entities that will pay Continental, and subsequently Aurora, for this technology. Efficiency and lower costs than what is best available today are the values fundamental values that motor carrier CEOs are focused on. Conventionally today, they provide enormously safe transport on our nation's roadways. What they would like to do is to provide even better more efficient and more affordable transport services so as to help energize the economy while they strive to improve sustainability. They also wish to achieve this without fanfare or distraction that can do nothing but disrupt efficiency and degrade service. So, hopefully, this design camouflage the LiDARs, and the fact that the cab is empty and focuses on transporting or repositioning its trailer efficiently, affordably and unremarkably. If it does this, it will be pass the "proof-of-market", and its value will be heard loud and clear by motor carrier CEOs, Continental and Chris! It's time. Alain</p>

<h3 class="newsletter-article-title" id="article-3"><a href="https://www.forbes.com/sites/danpontefract/2024/01/04/carrefour-drops-pepsi-products-continues-its-purpose-leadership/?sh=2c551cb14549" rel="noopener noreferrer">Carrefour Drops Pepsi Products, Continues Its Purpose Leadership</a></h3>
<p>D. Pontefract, Feb 4. "In another of its bold moves that underscores the company's commitment to purpose-driven and ethical business practices, French grocery giant <a href="https://www.carrefour.com/en" rel="noopener noreferrer">Carrefour</a> has halted the sale of Pepsi products due to unacceptable price increases. The move is in addition to their late 2023 decision to physically highlight the sneaky tactic of "shrinkflation" in their grocery stores. This two-pronged approach by France's second-largest grocer is not just a retail strategy; it's a loud message about the role of corporations in society and the importance of purpose-driven, ethical leadership.</p>
<p>The decision to <a href="https://www.reuters.com/markets/us/carrefour-says-it-will-not-sell-pepsico-goods-due-price-hikes-2024-01-04/" rel="noopener noreferrer">stop selling</a> Pepsi products comes amid rising food prices that have hit consumers hard. PepsiCo's price hikes, which the company attributes to increasing costs, have led Carrefour to draw a line in the grocery aisle, prioritizing consumer interests over supplier relationships. In a <a href="https://www.bnnbloomberg.ca/carrefour-pulls-pepsico-products-from-shelves-in-price-dispute-1.2017933" rel="noopener noreferrer">statement</a>, PepsiCo retaliated: "We've been in discussion with Carrefour for many months and we will continue to engage in good faith in order to try to ensure that our products are available."</p>
<p>Carrefour's move is significant, signalling a refusal to pass on undue cost burdens to customers. It's a stance that echoes a broader and global societal concern about the rising cost of living, and Carrefour is effectively using its market influence to advocate for more reasonable pricing practices. Of note, in Canada, a parliamentary committee has been working on a "<a href="https://canadacode.org/" rel="noopener noreferrer">grocery code of conduct</a>" to avoid such situations, with various grocery CEOs being called to parliamentarian committees to explain their pricing practices. (It's not going all that well for the CEOs.)</p>
<p>Carrefour's previous 2023 campaign against shrinkflation—reducing product size while maintaining or increasing prices—further cements the firm's position as a champion of transparency and fairness. By openly flagging products that have succumbed to this practice, including those from PepsiCo, Carrefour is not just advocating for fair pricing but also for honest communication with consumers. This dual approach to addressing price hikes and shrinkflation demonstrates a comprehensive commitment to their customers…. "<br/>
<a href="https://www.forbes.com/sites/danpontefract/2024/01/04/carrefour-drops-pepsi-products-continues-its-purpose-leadership/?sh=2c551cb14549" rel="noopener noreferrer">Read More</a> </p><p class="newsletter-commentary">Hmmmm…. While somewhat off-topic, this is the best news about corporate practices in a long time. This is a fundamental desire for value for the end customer by the intermediary. (This is similar to how Continental will help Aurora be successful). Carrefour is making Pepsi realize that delivering less value and less affordability has its limits even though the short run self-centered greed looked pretty good. Thank you, Carrefour. To many, Pepsi is 2nd rate, and eating Doritos is nothing but bad. So this is a win-win. For Carrefour: better other things to sell; for the consumer: better healthier items to buy.. Yea!! Alain</p>

<p>Press release, Dec. 29, Kia America announced today that it is unveiling an additional security measure to support its customers in response to criminals using methods of theft popularized on social media to steal or attempt to steal certain vehicle models. This new hardware modification—an ignition cylinder protector—is designed for the vehicles that are not eligible for the security software upgrade that Kia introduced earlier this year and works to combat theft by reinforcing the ignition cylinder body and preventing its removal through the method of theft promoted in videos that have spread across social media encouraging criminal activity.</p>
<p>This additional modification will be available beginning December 20 through a free Customer Satisfaction Initiative for the models that are not eligible for the software upgrade. <p>In addition to this ignition cylinder protector, Kia has also designed a software upgrade that further enhances a vehicle's security by restricting the operation of a vehicle's ignition system should a potential criminal attempt to steal a locked vehicle without the key, and the company continues to strongly encourage eligible customers to have this software upgrade installed. Since January, dealers have installed the anti-theft software upgrade on close to 940,000 vehicles, and Kia has hosted multiple temporary software upgrade clinics to assist local dealers with installation in key cities with many more planned in early 2024…<br/>
