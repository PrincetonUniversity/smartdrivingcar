<h3 class="newsletter-article-title" id="article-4"><a href="https://www.detroitnews.com/story/business/autos/2026/07/28/i-wouldnt-be-driving-without-it-seniors-adopt-self-driving-teslas/91059037007/" rel="noopener noreferrer">Forget
 rideshare. How seniors are using Teslas for transportation</a></h3>
<p>Henry
 Payne, July 9, "Tesla is expanding its Robotaxi rideshare service to include Florida, Texas, and California as it competes with Alphabet's Waymo, Amazon's Zoox, and other autonomous rideshare services. But consumers are already buying retail Teslas to self-drive
 them in cities across the United States.</p>
<p>Senior citizens most notably.</p>
<p>Determined to maintain their individual transportation independence, they are using Tesla's hands-free Full Self Driving system as a driver assist. Though by no means exclusive to seniors in its ability to add another set of eyes to the road, FSD helps elderly
 drivers maintain a cocoon around their car as their confidence in reflexes and eyesight naturally deteriorate.</p>
<p>"It's as good a driver as I am," said Dick Amacher, 78, a retired General Motors engineer from Rochester Hills who drives his Model Y and Model 3 on FSD 98% of the time. "Here's the thing: It will not go through a red light. It will not go through a stop sign.
 Every once in a while, I've driven through neighborhoods where stop signs were partially obscured by low-hanging branches, for example ... and (the Tesla) sees them before I do." ..."    <a href="https://www.detroitnews.com/story/business/autos/2026/07/28/i-wouldnt-be-driving-without-it-seniors-adopt-self-driving-teslas/91059037007/" rel="noopener noreferrer">Read
 more</a></p><p class="newsletter-commentary">Hmmmm…  That's what I've been doing all this year,
 and why some of us are desperately trying to get NJ MVC to create a tiered operator's license in NJ. One that would enable those who can't perform the "20 millisecond" aspects of the Dynamic Driving Task (DDT) as well as they did or should do, but can perform
 all the other aspects just fine. Creating tiers would allow more people to continue to have the mobility privilege that they've enjoyed since they were 16, if only they had (supervised) FSD or its equivalent in the car that they were permitted to be a user.
 Seems like such a no-brainer to delivering both improved safety and quality of life. Alain</p>

<p>Reuters, July 30, " Amazon's Zoox unit has received US approval for limited commercial deployment of its steering-wheel-free robotaxis,

 emerged.</p>
<p>Zoox's electric carriage-style vehicle features two rows of inward-facing seats and has a top speed of 75 miles per hour. Other companies, including Tesla and Alphabet's Waymo, are also racing to expand autonomous ride-hailing services in the United States.</p>
