<h3 class="newsletter-article-title" id="article-1"><a href="https://www.nytimes.com/2023/02/15/business/missy-cummings-tesla-autopilot.html" rel="noopener noreferrer">Carmakers Are Pushing Autonomous Tech. This Engineer Wants Limits.</a></h3>
<p>C. Metz, Feb. 15, Last fall, Missy Cummings sent a document to her colleagues at the National Highway Traffic Safety Administration that revealed a surprising trend: When people using advanced driver-assistance systems die or are injured in a car crash, they are more likely to have been speeding than people driving cars on their own.</p>
<p>The two-page analysis of nearly 400 crashes involving systems like Tesla's Autopilot and General Motors' Super Cruise is far from conclusive. But it raises fresh questions about the technologies that have been installed in hundreds of thousands of cars on U.S. roads. Dr. Cummings said the data indicated that drivers were becoming too confident in the systems' abilities and that automakers and regulators should restrict when and how the technology was used.</p>
<p>People "are over-trusting the technology," she said. "They are letting the cars speed. And they are getting into accidents that are seriously injuring them or killing them." </p>

<p>SafeDrivingCars: Those in which the automated function are explicitly designed to not only substantially reduce driver misbehavior by constraining the performance characteristic such as incorporating <a href="https://www.google.com/search?q=speed+governor&amp;rlz=1C1GCEB_enUS791US791&amp;oq=speed+governor&amp;aqs=chrome..69i57j0i512l9.4112j0j7&amp;sourceid=chrome&amp;ie=UTF-8" rel="noopener noreferrer">speed governors</a> that only permit excessive speeds in geofenced locations such as <a href="https://www.theglen.com/" rel="noopener noreferrer">Watkins Glen International</a> and stretches of the <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autobahn" rel="noopener noreferrer">German Autobahn</a>, but also automatically intervene to prevent crashes; thus, extending what is done today with <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-lock_braking_system" rel="noopener noreferrer">anti-lock brakes</a> and <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_stability_control" rel="noopener noreferrer">electronic stability control</a>.</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>SelfDrivingCars: Those which allow the driver for some extended period of time to be "feet-off" the brake &amp; throttle, delivering to the driver substantial comfort &amp; convenience, but also "hands-off" the wheel for shorter periods of time providing a little more comfort &amp; convenience. Absolutely required are "eyes &amp; brain" focused the human task of driving, ready to intervene should the automated driver begin to fail.</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>DriverlessCars: Those which serve only passengers and/or goods from trip start, through finish.
