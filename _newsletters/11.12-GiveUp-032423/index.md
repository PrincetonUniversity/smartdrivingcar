<p class="newsletter-intro">12h edition of the 11th year of SmartDrivingCars eLetter</p>
<p><a href="https://fnc.itu.int/programme/" rel="noopener noreferrer">ITU 2023 Future Networked Car Symposium</a>: <a href="https://fnc.itu.int/2023-session-3/" rel="noopener noreferrer">Automated Driving Systems (ADS) for Consumers and Other Vehicles (Trucks, Delivery, Shuttles, Robotaxis, Etc.)</a></p>
<p>R. Lanctot, March 12, "Full automation of the driving task appears tantalizingly close. Multiple use cases are emerging simultaneously, revealing potential paths to market adoption and consumer acceptance. The evolution of these use cases will determine the future of ADS. This panel will review the emerging ADS applications – consumer vehicles, commercial vehicles, delivery vehicles, shuttles, robotaxis – to better understand the challenges and opportunities associated with ADS technology and the state of development and market adoption…."</p>
<p><a href="https://fnc.itu.int/2023-session-3/" rel="noopener noreferrer">Read more</a> </p><p class="newsletter-commentary">Hmmmm….. After almost 15 years of substantive testing (<a href="https://www.google.com/url?sa=t&amp;rct=j&amp;q=&amp;esrc=s&amp;source=web&amp;cd=&amp;cad=rja&amp;uact=8&amp;ved=2ahUKEwi80dOUlvP9AhX-LFkFHfuSDbcQFnoECAsQAQ&amp;url=https%3A%2F%2Fwaymo.com%2F&amp;usg=AOvVaw0JOut92_774RXRlB10eL6H" rel="noopener noreferrer">the Google effort started in 2009</a>) and almost 20 years since <a href="https://www.google.com/url?sa=t&amp;rct=j&amp;q=&amp;esrc=s&amp;source=web&amp;cd=&amp;cad=rja&amp;uact=8&amp;ved=2ahUKEwiWvKaplvP9AhWcEFkFHSSjCu4QFnoECBoQAQ&amp;url=https%3A%2F%2Fen.wikipedia.org%2Fwiki%2FDARPA_Grand_Challenge_(2004)%26usg%3DAOvVaw1oN6pc6T_uG7n5q7Y2nzFU&amp;data=05%7C01%7Calaink%40princeton.edu%7C13fad823e178403133e808db2c3631ef%7C2ff601167431425db5af077d7791bda4%7C0%7C0%7C638152384866293876%7CUnknown%7CTWFpbGZsb3d8eyJWIjoiMC4wLjAwMDAiLCJQIjoiV2luMzIiLCJBTiI6Ik1haWwiLCJXVCI6Mn0%3D%7C3000%7C%7C%7C&amp;sdata=reXSMmSBuJlq8apjbGvTW%2BlcR3lJbV34To7h1oQBKIE%3D&amp;reserved=0" rel="noopener noreferrer">the first DARPA Challenge</a>, we are still only "… revealing potential paths to market adoption and consumer acceptance …"???</p>
<p class="newsletter-commentary">Isn't it about time that this teenager start delivering some tangible return to its "parents" and society. For what is supposed to be such a disruptive technology it has yet to identify the market where it has <a href="https://www.azquotes.com/author/13166-Joseph_A_Schumpeter?p=1" rel="noopener noreferrer">decisive cost or quality advantage over the existing firms</a>. (over the existing solutions it is trying to replace.)</p>
<p class="newsletter-commentary">It might be as safe as good drivers (It might be safer than bad drivers), but it has no chance anytime soon to being disruptively safer. It is not disruptively more fun to drive. Just ride around with it, that's a service, not a possession. It has no chance at being a consumer vehicle.</p>
<p class="newsletter-commentary">ADS has equally no chance at replacing commercial vehicle drivers. Helping professional drivers have an enhanced workplace? Yes! Removing them from their workplace? No!</p>
<p class="newsletter-commentary">Some special purposed deliveries in the middle of the night? Maybe.</p>
<p class="newsletter-commentary">Shuttles… at best a very small one-off niche with no opportunity to be disruptive.</p>
<p class="newsletter-commentary">Robotaxis designed and operated so as to serve rich ride-hailers and the chauffeured limousine market? Good luck! Service quality is really important and price is essentially irrelevant (these folks are rich and/or are traveling on an expense account). It is a non-trivial challenge for Robotaxis to deliver service quality approaching that of Uber/Lyft/Limo; so at best, these Robotaxis can only nip at the heels of Uber/Lyft/Limo, which itself is way less than 1% of the daily vehicle person-trips under 50 miles in length. Even if Robotaxis got'em all, there's nowhere near enough to justify any continued investment here.</p>
<p class="newsletter-commentary">These conundrums are NOT what was discussed in this session.</p>
<p class="newsletter-commentary">Unfortunately, what was also not discussed or realized is that there does exist an enormous market for demand-responsive Robotaxi service that is affordable. 50% of the people in the U.S. are not physically able to drive a car, or are not financially able to own one for themselves. They still need to get to work, to shops, to medical and rehab facilities, to school, to friends, to … and the fact that they cannot readily and affordably affects their well-being and the health of the entire country. This is a huge market where a demand-responsive and affordable service is disruptive because it delivers mobility to those who need a ride but are not being served by any transport alternatives which they can afford. THAT'S WHERE DRIVERLESS MAKES THE DIFFERENCE! Affordability is really important to those who are paying for their own travel and are non-rich. Thus, affordable, demand responsive Robotaxi service can readily be the best consumer choice for that 50%.</p>
<p class="newsletter-commentary">The addressable market here is ~150M people over the age of 10, wishing to make ~500M person trips a day in the USA that don't have their own car waiting around for them to drive it to take them where they want to go at <a href="https://www.google.com/url?sa=t&amp;rct=j&amp;q=&amp;esrc=s&amp;source=web&amp;cd=&amp;cad=rja&amp;uact=8&amp;ved=2ahUKEwjQn6fEqvT9AhUdElkFHQVRDCAQFnoECAsQAQ&amp;url=https%3A%2F%2Fdictionary.cambridge.org%2Fus%2Fdictionary%2Fenglish%2Fat-the-drop-of-a-hat&amp;usg=AOvVaw2s9u7G14d3iZaEZIzY__Pt" rel="noopener noreferrer">the drop of a hat</a>. To offer them with on-demand affordable Robotaxis service that is almost as well (and maybe even better) as if they did have that personal car, would be very disruptive to some, even many and eventually "all". A well-managed (100 person trips per day per Robotaxi, 20% profit margin) fleet of 5M Robotaxis could serve essentially all. Serving 1% would need 50,000. Serving a targeted opportunity representing the first 0.001% would require 50. This panel made no mention of this use case. No mention of the business case.</p>
<p class="newsletter-commentary">Let's continue this panel discussion at the <a href="https://www.cartsmobility.com/summit-sponsorship" rel="noopener noreferrer">6th Princeton SmartDrivingCars Summit in May 22-&gt;24</a>. A major part of the Summit is devoted to this one topic: Delivering Mobility to the Non-mobile. We're going to talk about the business case, something that is sorely missing in public discussion so far. Alain</p>
