<h3 class="newsletter-article-title" id="austin-is-fine-without-uber-and-lyft-until-it-isnt"><a href="https://techcrunch.com/2017/03/12/austin-is-fine-without-uber-and-lyft-until-it-isnt/" rel="noopener noreferrer">Austin is fine without Uber and Lyft… until it isn't</a></h3>
<p>F. Tepper, Mar 12,"Since Uber and
Lyft left last May, a bevy of
alternatives have sprung up — like
<a href="http://www.rideaustin.com/" rel="noopener noreferrer">Ride Austin</a>, <a href="https://fasten.com/" rel="noopener noreferrer">Fasten</a>
and <a href="http://www.ridefare.com/" rel="noopener noreferrer">Fare</a>. These</p>
<p>apps all essentially provide the
same experience as Uber and Lyft —
drop a pin and a car shows up. All
of them comply with Austin's <a href="https://techcrunch.com/2016/05/07/uber-and-lyft-shutdown-in-austin-after-voters-defeat-proposition-1/" rel="noopener noreferrer">background-check law</a>, and some of them even
charge drivers and riders less
than Uber and Lyft did.</p>
<p>I had even begun to wonder if
maybe Uber and Lyft made a mistake
by stubbornly refusing to comply
with Austin's fingerprint-based
background-check requirement,
because it showed that a major
U.S. city could actually survive
without them.</p>
<p>Until they couldn't.</p>
<p>Last night, arguably the biggest
night of SXSW, it rained —
and everyone wanted a ride. And on
cue, the apps failed. Overloaded
with demand, Ride Austin and
Fasten were essentially "bricked"
— you either got stuck at a
loading screen or the apps said
there were no cars available —
when there clearly were.</p>
<p>Riders were stuck, and drivers
