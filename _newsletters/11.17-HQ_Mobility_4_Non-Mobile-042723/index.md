<p>M. Sena, April 26, "I did some spring cleaning of my bookshelves. Before I chucked the ones I had picked out for removal, I leafed through them to be certain I was ready to part with them. Two are in the process of being re-read. One, The Accidental Century, was authored in 1965 by Michael Harrington. He was the founder of the Democratic Socialists of America. The book is a jewel. The second book, The Living End, was written by Roger Starr, a Democratic Realist, a life-long New Yorker, and an avid flyfisher. I have devoted a few pages of Dispatch Central to quotes from these two books.</p>
<p>Another book that has been on my shelves since 1997 is Clayton Christensen's The Innovator's Dilemma. I found my way back to it as a reference for a book that I am co-authoring with Professor Alain L. Kornhauser titled Mobility for the Non-mobile. Christensen devoted a whole chapter to why the car companies were getting it all wrong with electric cars—in 1997! There are great lessons for why everyone is getting it all wrong with driverless cars, which is the theme of the lead article.</p>
<p>The moral of this story: Don't be so quick to throw out old books; you never know when you are going to find them, read them, and appreciate what you didn't get the first time around..…." <a href="http://www.michaellsena.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/The-Dispatcher_May_2023-1.pdf" rel="noopener noreferrer">Read more</a> </p><p class="newsletter-commentary">Hmmmm….. Another enjoyable issue with a very serious message. Alain</p>
