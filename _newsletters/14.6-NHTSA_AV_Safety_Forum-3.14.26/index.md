<h3 class="newsletter-article-title" id="article-1"><a href="https://www.notateslaapp.com/news/3767/nhtsa-announces-it-will-reduce-regulations-for-autonomous-vehicles" rel="noopener noreferrer">NHTSA Announces It Will Reduce Regulations for Autonomous Vehicles</a></h3>
<p>"The regulatory ice surrounding fully autonomous vehicles is officially beginning to thaw.</p>
<p>Today, the National Highway Traffic Safety Administration (NHTSA) hosted its inaugural National AV Safety Forum in Washington, D.C., bringing together federal
 regulators and the top executives from Tesla, as well as other self-driving companies like Waymo, Zoox, and Aurora.</p>
<p>The tone of the summit was overwhelmingly pro-innovation. Rather than focusing on restrictive red tape, the discussions centered on how the federal government
 can actively modernize outdated automotive laws to pave the way for a new era of America-first transportation technology.
<a href="https://www.notateslaapp.com/news/3767/nhtsa-announces-it-will-reduce-regulations-for-autonomous-vehicles" rel="noopener noreferrer">Read more</a>  </p><p class="newsletter-commentary">Hmmmm…  The article is but
 a brief summary of the day as I experienced it.</p>
<p class="newsletter-commentary">The real substance of this outstanding day is contained in the
<a href="https://www.nhtsa.gov/events/av-public-meeting-2026" rel="noopener noreferrer">Agenda for the event</a> that includes individual links to videos of each of the sessions.
<a href="https://youtu.be/xSIgf5ztyqU" rel="noopener noreferrer">This is the link to the video of the entire event from start to finish</a>.</p>
<p class="newsletter-commentary">Especially
<a href="https://usdot.zoomgov.com/rec/play/Fe4yjGkXWpV_X6UgS_6j3n3M21MPZQLOD3cSMstdgvR7JLIPU6VRyhYuyYzC4KpLvJtLjotQh9v9cmTj.NIAYM18UaJBkVRAt?eagerLoadZvaPages=&amp;accessLevel=meeting&amp;canPlayFromShare=true&amp;from=share_recording_detail&amp;startTime=1773147985000&amp;componentName=rec-play&amp;originRequestUrl=https%3A%2F%2Fusdot.zoomgov.com%2Frec%2Fshare%2F5VDDoMyObWKRomaJrE6svbDvXkyo8p4PzmgtgVRsZd8Ww-BF2f9Yhi7DkcJxaBKm.VTIklEkx1XH9LWbc%3FstartTime%3D1773147985000" rel="noopener noreferrer">watch Secretary Duffy</a> emphasize not only the safety but also the affordability opportunity of this technology where he emphasized its opportunity to "…level the

<h3 class="newsletter-article-title" id="article-10"><a href="https://ggwash.org/view/102680/study-avs-will-super-charge-vmt" rel="noopener noreferrer">Study: AVs will super-charge vehicle miles traveled</a></h3>
<p>K. Wilson, Mar. 12, "..That could result not just in increased congestion, but also in bankrupt transit agencies, empty city coffers as fuel taxes and other
 driving-related revenues dip, and sprawling development patterns that leave us even more isolated from opportunity and each other than we are now.</p>
<p>And if those trips are taken in shared autonomous taxis, that would mean a lot of "deadhead" miles while empty AVs drive themselves to pick up new passengers
 — a phenomenon the study authors calls "eVMT," and which they warn could as much as double the number of miles an average city population drives in a day...."  <a href="https://ggwash.org/view/102680/study-avs-will-super-charge-vmt" rel="noopener noreferrer">Read more</a>
</p><p class="newsletter-commentary">Hmmmm… Assume the claim of increased congestion is true. My
 question is: Why disparage and blame the new riders via "roboTaxis" for the increased VMT and congestion, and not the base rides that exist today?  Those new rides unleashed happiness and improved quality-of-life to those new riders who today are forced to
 choose to stay home and not capture for themselves the happiness that affordable high-quality mobility would provide to them.  All those traveling today are fortunate enough to have a driver's license, be rich enough to own a car, have such good friends who
 will give them a ride, etc.  What about those who don't?  Shouldn't they have equal access to the capacity of public roadways?  Also, public transit is already bankrupt everywhere in the USA. And, the thought that there is no "empty vehicle repositioning"
 going on throughout the nation's roadways is absurd. Know anyone who drives their kids to school, or who picks them up from after school activity?  Has an Uber driver ever picked you up at the same spot they had just dropped off their previous rider?  In the
 morning, I claim that the Average vehicle occupancy in Princeton, NJ is 0.85 and throughout the day throughout New Jersey is 0.98.  What one sees as a driver is often just a machine, not a rider seeking mobility.</p>
<p class="newsletter-commentary">So happy these researchers are not from UT Austin.  Couldn't have happened there, because I can't imagine that Kara would have put up with such nonsense.
 Alain</p>
