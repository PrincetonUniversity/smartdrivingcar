<h3 class="newsletter-article-title" id="urban-mobility-system-upgrade-how-shared-self-driving-cars-could-change-city-traffic"><a href="http://orfe.princeton.edu/%7Ealaink/SmartDrivingCars/PDFs/15CPB_Self-drivingcars.pdf" rel="noopener noreferrer">Urban Mobility System Upgrade: How shared self-driving cars could change city traffic</a></h3>
<p>"What we found: Nearly the same mobility can be delivered with 10% of the cars" <a href="http://orfe.princeton.edu/%7Ealaink/SmartDrivingCars/PDFs/15CPB_Self-drivingcars.pdf" rel="noopener noreferrer">Read more</a> </p><p class="newsletter-commentary">Hmmm... You must read more. This is a very interesting study and I'm still digging through it; however, if true, then European cities are very different than New Jersey (and the rest of the US). In New Jersey, trips are very diffuse spatially and temporally such that unless trips are severely aggregate both spatially and temporally and "the peak is flattened" (meaning that it is no longer "the same mobility") there isn't sufficient ride-sharing during the peak to reduce the needed vehicles so drastically.</p>
<p class="newsletter-commentary">Shared-use allows the fleet to be reduced to those that are needed during the peak. Shared-ride during the peak allows the peak fleet to be reduced. I question that combined these lead to an order of magnitude reduction in needed vehicles unless people in Europe own many cars that they never use. Alain</p>

<h3 class="newsletter-article-title" id="state-wide-ataxi-service"><a href="http://www.princeton.edu/%7Ealaink/Orf467F14/Orf467F14FinalProjectPresentationSchedule.pdf" rel="noopener noreferrer">State-wide aTaxi Service</a></h3>
<p>Serving New Jersey's Personal Mobility Needs with the Casual Sharing of autonomousTaxis (aTaxis): Land Use, Personal Travel Demand, Casual Ride-sharing Potential, Fleet Size Requirements and Empty aTaxi Management. Orf 467F14 Student Reports for each of New Jersey's counties.</p>
<p>Even though these are unedited student reports they contain substantial information and perspectives on the opportunities and challenges for aTaxis to adequately serve the majority of New Jersey's mobility needs. While there are significant causal ride sharing opportunities during peak hours in peak directions, sufficient to eliminate congestion, and aTaxis can substantially increase the utilization (by a multiple of 5) of NJ Transit's rail system by providing efficient and high quality "last mile" mobility to/from rail stations, it is unlikely that average vehicle occupancy (AVO = personTripMiles/aTaxiMiles) for all trips, all day could reach a level of 2.0. Moreover, that is before one accounts for empty aTaxi repositioning that enables the sharing of aTaxis. These reports are the first that begin to address the empty aTaxis problem. It is non-trivial especially if one wishes to have multiple sized aTaxis so as to have the size of the serving aTaxi more closely match the number of shared rides that are being served. The spatial-temporal imbalances of the shared-ride demand seem to impose a great deal of empty repositioning.</p>
<p>It may well turn out that having only four passenger aTaxis may be better than having a fleet consisting of 2 and 6 passenger aTaxis. The reduction in empty repositioning of a uniform fleet of aTaxis may well compensate for the extra energy consumed by a larger aTaxi when a smaller two passenger aTaxis could have done the job and the foregoing of some shared riding when two four passenger aTaxis need to be used because a single 6 passenger aTaxis was not available to serve the six travelers. Alain January 4, 2015</p>
