<h3 class="newsletter-article-title" id="article-1"><a href="https://www.nytimes.com/2023/08/21/technology/waymo-driverless-cars-san-francisco.html" rel="noopener noreferrer">'This Experience May Feel Futuristic': Three Rides in Waymo Robot Taxis</a></h3>
<p>T. Mickie, Aug 21, "Hello, Tripp," a disembodied woman's voice said through the speakers of a driverless taxi that was about to pick up a fare near the colorful Victorian houses known as the Painted Ladies.</p>
<p>"This experience may feel futuristic," the voice said. "Please don't touch the steering wheel or pedals during the ride. For any questions, you can find information in the Waymo app, like how we keep our cars safe or clean."</p>
<p>For several years, San Francisco's hilly and congested streets have doubled as a test track for hundreds of driverless cars operated by <a href="https://www.nytimes.com/2021/04/02/business/waymo-john-krafcik.html" rel="noopener noreferrer">Waymo, an autonomous vehicle company owned by the Google parent company Alphabet</a>, and <a href="https://www.nytimes.com/2022/09/28/technology/driverless-cars-san-francisco.html" rel="noopener noreferrer">General Motors-owned Cruise</a>…. </p>
<p><a href="https://www.nytimes.com/2023/08/21/technology/waymo-driverless-cars-san-francisco.html" rel="noopener noreferrer">Read more</a> </p><p class="newsletter-commentary">Hmmmm… Although I included this article in the body of SDC 11.32, I am repeating it here so that I can include a letter-to-the editor that I sent the NY Times, which is unlikely that the NYT will publish, so I'll just include it here as follows…</p>
<p class="newsletter-commentary">To the editor:</p>
<p class="newsletter-commentary">While I applaud the Times for their seriousness of purpose in sending three reporters to determine, for themselves, if the driverless rides offered by Waymo in San Francisco actually work without smoke, mirrors, or snake oil, and for their un-sensationalized, balanced assessment, it is a shame that the PR around the enhanced quality-of-life opportunity for this form of mobility remains overlooked.</p>
<p class="newsletter-commentary">Instead of focusing on the tourist experience for those who already have the wherewithal of time, money, and physical ability to be chauffeured around, I recommend that for the next article you get the opinion of a formerly incarcerated individual who could have a night job, but can't get there because <a href="https://www.sfmta.com/getting-around/muni/routes-stops/weekday-frequency-guide" rel="noopener noreferrer">Muni operates</a> but 2 bus routes between midnight and 5 am, or a single parent who can't leave work to give their kids a ride to the doctor or to cello lessons. Or to the many people who find themselves in the unenviable position of having to ask a friend, let alone a stranger, for a ride. I urge you to talk with people whose lives would be fundamentally improved if they had easy access to safe, equitable, demand responsive, affordable mobility 24/7/365, just like, maybe even better, than having their own car to drive themselves.</p>
<p class="newsletter-commentary">By the way, we compute that in the 49 square miles of San Francisco there is a demand for about 2.1 million person trips every day. About 30% are well served affordably by Muni (but require a <a href="https://www.sfmta.com/blog/fiscal-years-2021-2022-budget-revenue-expenditures-and-future-projections" rel="noopener noreferrer">subsidy that is 4x the affordable fare</a>) and about 10% can afford the luxury of being chauffeured <a href="http://tncstoday.sfcta.org/" rel="noopener noreferrer">by Uber/Lyft</a>/taxi/limo. That leaves more than 1 million that prefer to drive themselves or get a ride from a family member, friend or neighbor or don't improve their lives by going because, Muni's rides aren't suitable, Uber/Lyft/taxi/limo are too expensive, or they can't convince someone to take them. Waymo (and Cruise) can safely serve all 2.1 million; however, they can make life changing differences to this last group that can't afford to go. With casual ridesharing, Waymo/Cruise could achieve a 100 personTrips/vehicleDay productivity and break-even average cost, without subsidy, of less than $2.50 /personTrip (~$2.43/personTrip, <a href="https://www.dropbox.com/scl/fi/9jkoqujvj4b2c7pl1obzp/CPUC-Testimony_v4.docx?rlkey=h69y49qhhrtly5xm75ypkuv5l&amp;dl=0" rel="noopener noreferrer">Public Comment to the California Public Utility Commission, August 10, 2023 by Dr. Alain L. Kornhauser, Princeton University</a>, also attached).</p>
<p class="newsletter-commentary">Now that's high-quality, affordable mobility that delivers quality-of-life throughout the city. The city should be begging them to so enhance SF's Quality-of-life.</p>
<p class="newsletter-commentary">Alain L. Kornhauser, PhD</p>
